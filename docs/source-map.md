# Source and assumption map

## Exercise baseline

The supplied take-home PDF sets a hypothetical starting point of 1 billion weekly active users, about 150,000 weekly support issues, and 60% automated resolution. It asks for a three-year outlook, strategic priorities, a future operating model, and measures of success. The exercise PDF stays outside this public repository.

## Planning assumptions

The recommendation uses illustrative Year 3 factors of 1.5× weekly users and 2.22× issues per user. The logic is that a larger user base raises demand, while more products and business use cases could raise issue frequency. Those factors produce 500,000 weekly issues in Year 3. Verified AI resolution rises from the exercise's 60% starting point to 70%, 82%, and 90% in this stretch plan. The slower 82% Year 3 path would leave about 90,000 weekly human cases. These figures are scenario assumptions, separate from OpenAI targets. The chart depicts automated resolution and human assistance, while backlog remains outside its scope.

The 90% stretch case leaves about 50,000 weekly issues for people, compared with 60,000 today. Year 1 cases reaching people rise to 66,000, so temporary coverage is part of the plan. This path requires enough eligible issue volume, approved actions, durable resolution, and safe handoffs. It gives no credit for preventing issues through product fixes. As AI resolves more routine issues, the remaining human queue may include more sensitive cases and exceptions. Staffing decisions require observed case volume, coverage, backlog, customer outcomes, and cost. The recommended first move is to shift people toward harder cases, quality review, and product fixes. Proposed pilots for invoice lookup and product how-to guidance require a Day 30 volume and risk review. The Day 90 targets of a 5-point durable-resolution gain, 10% fewer eligible cases reaching people, and zero severe errors are illustrative gates, separate from OpenAI commitments.

## External source

[OpenAI's published support model](https://openai.com/index/openai-support-model/) informs the recommendation's connection between customer support surfaces, living knowledge, evaluations, and frontline feedback. It also supplies Glen Worthington's quoted description of the purpose of Support. The exercise supplies the hypothetical baseline, and my planning case supplies the multipliers.

## Albert's experience and the demonstration

Albert supplied four years of Meta BPO management across five onshore and offshore vendor partners, plus Meta scorecard measures for coverage, quality, workforce, sales funnel, and Ads AI adoption. The recommendation uses relevant measures inside four Support operating lenses and keeps business revenue as prioritization context. It makes no historical OpenAI Support performance claim.

The additional operating scenarios use fictional organizations, people, dates, goals, and records. One deterministic browser engine calculates their recommendations, and the visual agent chart replays proposed handoffs. The guide gives fixed answers from page content.

## Financial decision case

The [financial model notes](financial-model.md) document public vendor prices, the exercise comparison, all illustrative inputs, the $117.5M high-growth plan, the $13.1M downside shortfall, and a lower-demand sensitivity. The [calculation module](../src/finance.mjs) produces the unrounded figures. The model estimates vendor cost avoided against a flat-automation forecast and gives no dollar credit for revenue or customer retention.
