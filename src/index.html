CORE 01 / THE CASE FOR CHANGE

Support at 1 billion weekly users

Lots of organizations deal with scale. Fewer deal with scale and hypergrowth.The exercise starts with 150,000 support issues each week. People handle 60,000 of them.My three-year planning scenario reaches 500,000 weekly issues while AI cuts the share handled by people from 40% today to 10% in Year 3.

Imagine a World Where:

2 billionweekly users
500kweekly issues
90%automated resolution
50khuman-assisted issues
The three-year vision

AI transforms Support from a queue that just responds to issues, to a connected system that 1) anticipates needs, 2) resolves approved problems inside the product, and 3) learns from every exception.

My recommendation
  1. Build toward 90% automated resolution by Year 3 as a stretch target. That moves the human share of weekly issues from 40% to 10%.
  2. Use trained people for complex cases, AI quality review, and fixes to repeat issues.
  3. Change planned vendor staffing only after the agreed Service Level Agreement (SLA) target and service coverage hold.

The starting figures come from the exercise. The three-year path is my planning scenario, separate from any OpenAI target.

CORE 03 / THE PLAN

Change the product, people, and process together.

Fund shared issue data and quality checks first, then expand AI only on journeys that pass customer and safety checks. This delays some early savings but protects service as the remaining cases grow harder.

PRODUCT · FIRST INVESTMENT

Resolve more inside the product

  • Product and Engineering connect account context, approved guidance, and limited AI actions
  • Start with invoice lookup and product guidance, then fix repeat issues found by Support and Product Engagement
  • Trust & Safety approves sensitive action boundaries before launch
PEOPLE · SECOND INVESTMENT

Build specialist and partner skills

  • Support Delivery trains and checks vendor teams for defined queues, AI exceptions, and clean handoffs
  • Specialists handle novel, sensitive, and complex cases and review AI failures
  • Frontline teams turn recurring failures into quality tests and Product fixes
PROCESS · SHARED FOUNDATION

Run one learning loop

  • Operations keeps one issue ID from AI through partner or specialist resolution
  • Approved knowledge, weekly case reviews, and quality tests control expansion
  • A severe error stops the affected AI action, sends cases to people, and triggers a fix
First 90 days
  • Audit: Map issue types, AI-ready volume, vendor capacity, and service gaps
  • Align: Name decision owners, one issue ID, and pilot success measures
  • Test: Run invoice lookup and product guidance with human escalation
Year 1 · prove
  • Release checked AI help in high-volume journeys
  • Certify partner teams for exceptions and handoffs
  • Target 70% automated resolution and plan about 1,030 modeled vendor seats
Year 2 · expand
  • Add approved actions across products and channels
  • Use failures to update knowledge and product flows
  • Target 80% automated resolution and plan about 1,030 modeled vendor seats
Year 3 · redesign
  • Make AI the first route for eligible needs with clear human escalation
  • Shift people toward complex cases and quality review
  • Target 90% automated resolution and plan about 780 seats if SLA and coverage hold

Why those pilots? Invoice lookup and product guidance have clear answers and human escalation. The first audit would confirm demand and risk before launch.

How the seats were modeled. Year 3 uses 500k weekly issues × 10% reaching people × 52 weeks × 90% vendor share ÷ 3,000 cases per rep yearly = 780 seats. This is a scenario estimate, not OpenAI staffing.

The model sets aside an illustrative $20M across three years for AI use, product integration, training, and quality checks. The yearly planning amounts are $4M, $6M, and $10M as rollout grows. Finance would price the two 90-day pilots and replace this estimate before an investment decision.

The model already uses 30% onshore and 70% offshore vendor costs ?. That mix is included in every case. See the inputs and public price references.

Separate location option

Moving 100 suitable vendor seats from onshore to offshore could save about $7.0M yearly before transition costs. That is separate from AI savings. The high case starts with about 940 modeled vendor seats, and offshore shifts carry quality risk in language, specialist accuracy, and escalation.

What I need from leadership
  • Approval for two bounded pilots. Test invoice lookup and product guidance, with a clear route to a person, so we can measure customer outcomes before expansion.
  • Interoperability alignment. Give Product, Engineering, Trust & Safety, Support Delivery, Operations, and Product Engagement named owners for shared issue history and approval decisions.
  • A priced pilot budget. Have Finance cost AI usage, integration, training, and review before setting the rollout budget.
How I would judge progress
  • Day 90 pilot gate. Seek 10% fewer eligible cases reaching people, a five-point improvement in checked resolution, and zero severe AI errors.
  • Annual path. Check automated resolution against 70%, 80%, and 90%. Keep the human-case path at 66k, 66k, and 50k weekly.
  • Quarterly decision. Expand only while repeat contact, SLA, and backlog stay on target. Keep staffing if those results slip. Count savings only when Finance confirms lower vendor spend.

Starting point. The exercise supplies 1 billion weekly users, about 150k weekly issues, and a 60% automated resolution rate.

Demand growth. This stretch case assumes weekly users double over three years. Paid, business, and developer use can also raise issue frequency from 1.5 to 2.5 per 10,000 weekly users. Together those assumptions produce 500k weekly issues in Year 3. The yearly steps are planning estimates.

Case mix. Separate repeatable questions, approved account actions, complex billing and access cases, and sensitive policy or safety cases. AI takes approved routine issues. Trained partners and specialists take the harder remainder.

90% feasibility. Automated resolution counts all started issues that finish without a person, excluding abandonment. As a math check, 95% of issues would need to be safe for automation and 95% of those would need a checked resolution to reach about 90% overall. If the first audit finds a smaller eligible share, I would lower the target and keep human coverage.

Slower AI progress. The low case holds Year 3 automated resolution at 80%, leaving about 100k weekly cases for people. This is an illustrative downside, not a forecast.

Staffing. Modeled vendor seats rise from about 940 today to 1,030 in Year 1, stay near 1,030 in Year 2, then fall to 780 in Year 3. These are workload estimates, not current OpenAI headcount. Actual staffing depends on case mix, SLA, quality, and existing vendor arrangements.

APPENDIX B / OPERATING DETAIL

One issue history from start to finish.

01Spot or capture

Operations assigns one issue ID and records the issue type, product context, and prior attempts.

Example. A customer opens an invoice question from their account with the invoice date and earlier help steps attached.

02Resolve or route

Product and Trust & Safety approve the AI action. Support Delivery owns handoffs to trained partners or specialists when the answer is uncertain.

Example. AI finds an invoice under an approved account action. An unmatched charge goes to a billing specialist with the full history.

03Verify and improve

Operations checks sampled outcomes and repeat contact. Support Delivery coaches partners. Product and Engineering fix recurring causes and add failures to AI quality tests.

Example. Repeat invoice questions reveal confusing labels, so Product changes the billing page and Operations updates the guidance.

How results are counted. Use one issue ID and compare similar issue types. During pilots, compare the new route with comparable issues on the current route. Count AI or self-service as automated only when the requested outcome is checked and no related contact returns within seven days. Sample exits for unanswered issues. Report automated resolution, partner quality and SLA, specialist outcomes, and repeat issue volume separately. A rising recontact rate or severe error pauses expansion. Missed SLA or backlog targets pause staffing reductions.
