01 / THE FUTURE OF SUPPORT AT MASSIVE SCALE

Support at 1 billion weekly users

Lots of organizations deal with scale. Fewer deal with scale and hypergrowth.The exercise already starts with 150,000 issues and 60,000 human-assisted cases each week. A small increase in issue frequency creates a large amount of additional demand.In my three-year planning case, issues approach 281,000 each week. If verified automation reaches only 75%, the harder human queue requires about 52% more effort, raising the risk of slower answers and repeat contacts. I would aim for a higher verified resolution rate while keeping people in control of sensitive decisions.

The vision What this looks like in 3 years

Support spots common needs and resolves approved problems inside the product before they become tickets, while people govern AI, handle sensitive cases, and remove the causes of repeat issues.

My recommendation

Use an illustrative path from 60% to 88% verified automated resolution by Year 3. At modeled demand, human cases fall from 60k to about 34k weekly and human effort falls from 60k to 44k units; I would redeploy released capacity first.

01 / FUTURE SCALE · DEMAND AND CAPABILITY

AI takes more cases as human cases fall.

My stretch case assumes weekly issues rise from 150k to 281k and the average human case becomes 1.3× harder. Verified automated resolution rises in stages to 88%, which is enough to lower both human case volume and human effort.

About 84% is the effort break-even point

At Year 3 demand and 1.3× effort per human case, verified automation must exceed about 84% to bring human effort below today's level.

88% is the stretch case

Human cases reach about 34k weekly and effort reaches about 44k units, 27% below today. At 75% automation, effort would rise to about 91k units.

Capacity must change with proof

Keep qualified coverage while AI takes on more work. Redeploy released hours into review, difficult cases, and product fixes; reset staffing through attrition or partner contracts only after durable resolution, backlog, and safety hold.

Capability must change first

Give AI approved actions, current knowledge, tests, traces, and a fast human handoff. Train partners to handle exceptions and surface failures, while internal specialists own policy, evaluations, and high-risk decisions.

Scenario logic Year 3 uses 1.5× weekly users, 1.25× issues per user, and 1.3× effort per human case. The path gives no credit for preventing issues; product fixes could improve demand further.

Eligibility is the gate. The 88% path requires enough issues to qualify for safe automated resolution; I would measure eligible volume by issue type before endorsing this target.

02 / STRATEGIC PRIORITIES

Three moves make the 88% path credible.

OpenAI's published support model describes connected support surfaces, living knowledge, evaluations, and frontline feedback. I would extend that system so it resolves more needs inside the product and uses verified outcomes to reduce repeat demand.

PRODUCT

Prevent repeat issues

Use support patterns to fix confusing flows and place help where customers get stuck.

Reward Fewer issues reach Support as usage grows.Risk to manage Confirm the product change actually lowers repeat contact.
AI ACTIONS

Complete approved work

Move beyond answers to bounded actions such as invoice lookup, with identity checks, audit trails, and human handoff.

Reward More issues stay resolved without a human ticket.Risk to manage A wrong action needs a stop rule and recovery path.
PEOPLE

Make frontline teams builders

Give partners and specialists time to flag failures, improve guidance, and create test cases.

Reward Each exception improves the next automated and human answer.Risk to manage Protect coverage while people learn these new tasks.
Investment order

Start with shared issue history, evaluations, and safe action tools. Expand AI coverage and change partner capacity only after the customer outcome holds for comparable cases.

03 / FUTURE OPERATING MODEL

AI resolves the routine; people govern the system.

One issue history follows the customer across product help, partners, and specialists. Support gives AI approved scope, and people keep clear approval, escalation, and rollback rights.

01Spot or capture the need

Use product context and prior attempts once.

02Resolve or route

AI handles approved work; unclear or sensitive cases reach a person with context.

03Verify and improve

Check the outcome, then update knowledge, tests, or the product.

AI and product help

Owns approved guidance and actions with traceable results.

Hands off low-confidence, sensitive, or failed cases.

Partner teams

Own defined human queues, coverage, and calibrated case reviews.

Improve guidance by flagging recurring customer failures.

Internal specialists

Own policy, high-risk judgment, AI release rules, and partner standards.

Drive repeat issue fixes with Product and Engineering.

How the partner model changes

Partner success shifts from ticket throughput to durable resolution, reliable coverage, and useful feedback. As routine volume falls, I would retrain and redeploy people first, then adjust contracted capacity after results remain stable.

Human review

Sample routine AI cases and require a person for sensitive actions.

Clear fallback

Carry full context into the human route when AI is uncertain.

Stop and recover

Pause an affected action after a severe error and review every impacted case.

04 / MEASURES OF SUCCESS

Scale AI only when customer outcomes hold.

The lead outcome is durable resolution by issue type. The customer gets the right result without returning with that issue during an appropriate review window. One issue ID keeps AI, partner, and specialist contributions visible across handoffs.

APPENDIX / PLANNING ASSUMPTIONS

The numbers behind the stretch case.

Starting point. The exercise supplies 1 billion weekly users, about 150k weekly issues, 60% automated resolution, and 40% human assistance.

Demand growth. User factors rise to 1.15×, 1.32×, and 1.50× across Years 1–3. Issues per user rise to 1.08×, 1.16×, and 1.25×. These are my illustrative assumptions.

Case complexity. Human effort per case rises to 1.10×, 1.20×, and 1.30× as repeatable issues leave the queue. One unit equals today's average human case.

AI path. Verified automated resolution rises to 70%, 80%, and 88% in this stretch case. The exercise provides no OpenAI target. Count a resolution only after quality and repeat-contact checks.

Effort threshold. At Year 3 volume, about 84% verified automation holds human effort below today's level. At 75%, human cases reach about 70k and effort about 91k units.

Year-by-year outlook. Today shows 150k issues, 60k human cases, and 60k effort units; Year 1 shows 186k, 56k, and 61k; Year 2 shows 230k, 46k, and 55k; Year 3 shows 281k, 34k, and 44k.

Staffing conversion. Effort units guide planning. Staffing requires observed handle time, qualified hours, occupancy, shrinkage, service windows, and a transition plan.

Pilot gates. Invoice lookup and product how-to guidance are proposed examples. The Day 90 gates depend on a Day 30 baseline, risk review, and an appropriate resolution window.

External source OpenAI's published support model. Its support surfaces, knowledge, evaluations, and frontline feedback inform this proposal.
