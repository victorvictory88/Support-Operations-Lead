OPENAI SUPPORT OPERATIONS / TAKE-HOME RECOMMENDATION

Build capacity through verified resolution.

My recommendation is to make every support path accountable for a durable customer outcome. Improve product help and AI for suitable issues, use partners for repeatable work, and reserve internal specialists for complex or sensitive cases. One operating team should own forecasting, quality, partner governance, and the feedback loop into Product and Engineering.

01 / THE OUTLOOK

Volume grows. Human effort grows faster.

EXPLICIT PLANNING ASSUMPTIONS

Year-three users grow 1.5×. Weekly issues per user rise 1.25× as products and customer needs expand. The average human case needs 1.3× today’s effort. A 75% verified automated-resolution rate is a conditional goal, subject to audits and user outcomes.

02 / PRIORITIES

Three investments, in dependency order.

Begin with a reliable baseline, then improve resolution paths and partner execution. Automated share should follow better outcomes.

01

Instrument the journey

Create a shared issue taxonomy across product, channel, region, customer segment, partner, and risk tier. Link automation attempts, human handoffs, repeat contacts, and final outcomes to one issue ID.

Expected effectExpose false closures and give workforce planning a case-mix baseline. Reported containment can hide unresolved users.
02

Improve the resolution system

Place help inside the product for common issues. Maintain versioned knowledge, test AI on representative cases, and let agents submit failed responses for review. Pilot low-risk actions behind approval and rollback controls.

Expected effect + tradeoffImprove durable resolution and reduce repeat demand. Ship fewer automations while evaluation coverage catches up.
03

Rebuild partner capacity

Run one vendor-management system across sites. Route stable cases to trained partners, cross-train for peaks, and protect specialist capacity for product defects, fraud, account risk, and escalations.

Expected effect + tradeoffReduce backlog swings and improve partner quality. Carry reserve capacity for launches and incidents.

PARTNER COMMERCIAL DESIGN

Pay for dependable capacity and verified outcomes.

Use a base capacity commitment with variable rewards for audited resolution and quality. Compare partners within equivalent case and risk mixes. Hold a weekly service review, a monthly cross-partner calibration, and a quarterly commercial review. Maintain a trained backup route for critical queues and test the transfer plan each quarter. Decide on supplier consolidation only after the baseline exposes overlap and risk.

ISO 18295 covers contact-centre service and client oversight. COPC’s customer-operations framework links forecasts, quality, knowledge, supplier management, and cost. 04 / SEQUENCE

Earn the right to scale each change.

Each phase produces evidence for the next investment. The plan can accelerate when pilots pass and slow when customer outcomes fall.

FIRST 90 DAYS

Find the baseline

Audit the 60% automated-resolution claim. Segment weekly issues by intent, risk, channel, region, and repeat contact. Build a weekly demand forecast and shared partner scorecard. Select two high-volume, low-risk journeys for pilots.

Exit evidenceIssue IDs join across channels, QA calibrates across teams, and pilots have comparison cohorts.
YEAR 1

Prove the system

Deploy versioned knowledge and AI evaluation. Pilot in-product help, agent assistance, and one approved action path. Rebalance partner queues after quality and staffing reviews. Establish specialist escalation and incident runbooks.

Exit evidenceDurable resolution improves without a higher severe-error rate or longer specialist backlog.
YEAR 2

Expand by journey

Extend successful patterns across languages and product surfaces. Cross-train partner sites, test backup routing, and use frontline defect clusters to prioritize Product and Engineering fixes. Renegotiate contracts around measured outcomes.

Exit evidenceCase-mix-adjusted partner performance stays stable through launches and regional peaks.
YEAR 3

Operate adaptively

Refresh forecasts monthly and after major launches. Route by skill and risk signals. Retire weak automations, expand tested ones, and allocate specialist capacity to emerging products and sensitive cases.

Exit evidenceService quality and backlog stay inside agreed limits at observed demand.
05 / MEASURES

Score the journey, then its contributors.

Leadership should review outcomes by customer segment, issue type, region, and risk. Give operational credit after durable resolution, with each team’s contribution visible in the linked issue record.

PAUSE

Any severe privacy, fraud, policy, or account-access error pauses the affected route and sends the case to its human owner.

INVESTIGATE

Two weekly cohorts with recontact above the comparison cohort or accuracy below it stop expansion while the team reviews cases.

EXPAND

Improved durable resolution with stable risk and backlog qualifies a journey for staged expansion and continued sampling.

Planning math

The exercise supplies 1B weekly users, 150k weekly issues, and 60% automated resolution. Year-three 1.5× user growth, 1.25× issue propensity, 1.3× human case effort, and 75% verified automated resolution are planning assumptions. They are sensitivity inputs, separate from forecasts or OpenAI targets.

At a constant 60% automated rate, 281,250 × 40% × 1.3 = 146,250 effort units. At 75%, 281,250 × 25% × 1.3 = 91,406 units. One unit equals today’s average human case effort. Staffing requires observed handle time, occupancy, shrinkage, service windows, and case mix before an FTE estimate.

In a stress case with 65% automated resolution and 1.5× human case effort, human effort reaches about 148k units. Maintain a backup capacity path until data narrows this exposure. Seven-day recontact is an initial measure. Longer case types need a longer outcome window.
