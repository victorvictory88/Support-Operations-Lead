CORE 01 / THE CASE FOR CHANGE

Support at 1 billion weekly users

Lots of organizations deal with scale. Fewer deal with scale and hypergrowth.The exercise starts with 150,000 support issues each week. People handle 60,000 of them.In my three-year planning scenario, weekly issues reach about 281,000. AI would need to resolve far more issues, while people handle the harder cases that remain.

1 billionweekly users
150kweekly issues
60%automated resolution

“Support has never really been about replying to just tickets. It’s about whether people get what they need, whether it actually serves them well.”

Glen Worthington, Head of User Ops
The three-year vision

Support spots needs inside the product, completes approved actions, and turns every exception into better guidance and product fixes. People own sensitive decisions and AI quality.

My recommendation

Plan toward 88% verified automated resolution as an illustrative stretch case. At modeled Year 3 demand, issues reaching people fall from 60k to about 34k each week. Move people toward harder cases, quality review, and repeat-issue fixes. Change staffing only after customer outcomes and coverage hold.

The starting figures come from the exercise. The three-year path is my planning scenario, separate from any OpenAI target.

CORE 03 / THE PLAN

Build the system before changing staffing.

These three investments run together. Each one reduces avoidable demand or makes the next AI and human resolution more reliable.

PRODUCT

Prevent repeat issues

Use support patterns to fix confusing product paths and place help where customers get stuck.

Expected gain Fewer issues enter Support.
AI ACTIONS

Complete approved tasks

Give AI bounded actions, current guidance, identity checks, and a full-context handoff to people.

Expected gain More eligible issues stay resolved.
PEOPLE

Turn exceptions into fixes

Give partners and specialists time to review failures, improve guidance, and test new paths.

Expected gain Hard cases improve the system.
AI and product help

Handle approved routine issues and keep an auditable record.

Partner teams

Handle defined exceptions, protect coverage, and flag repeat failures.

Internal specialists

Own sensitive decisions, policy, AI release rules, and product fixes.

Capacity decision

As AI handles more eligible issues, I would move people toward harder cases, quality review, and product fixes first. Training and backup coverage add cost early, so I would adjust staffing through attrition or partner contracts only after case volume, coverage, customer outcomes, and cost support the change.

CORE 03 / THE PLAN · PROOF AND DECISIONS

Prove each step before expanding AI.

First 90 days

Map eligible issues, record quality and cost baselines, and pilot invoice lookup and product how-to guidance. Decide on each pilot using lasting resolution, repeat contact, severe errors, and cases reaching people.

Year 1

Target 70% verified automation and about 56k weekly cases reaching people. Keep customer outcomes and backlog at least as strong as the baseline before expanding.

Year 2

Target 80% verified automation and about 46k weekly cases reaching people. Show lower cost per lasting resolution while repeat contact and backlog stay at or below baseline.

Year 3

Test 88% verified automation and about 34k weekly cases reaching people. Adjust vendor capacity only after two consecutive reviews confirm strong quality, reliable coverage, and lower cost.

Check every year Report verified AI resolution, cases reaching people, lasting resolution, repeat contact, severe errors, backlog age, and cost per lasting resolution by issue type. The numeric targets above are my planning assumptions, separate from OpenAI commitments.

What I need from leadership
  • Linked issue, repeat-contact, and cost data across channels.
  • Named Product, Engineering, and policy owners.
  • Training time and flexible partner terms.
How I would judge progress
  • Durable resolution and severe errors show customer quality.
  • Human case volume and backlog show capacity.
  • Cost per lasting resolution shows the full economics.
Decision rule

Track one issue across AI, partners, and internal specialists, and count a lasting resolution once. Stop an affected AI action after a severe error or rising repeat contact. Keep human coverage if queues stay high.

Starting point. The exercise supplies 1 billion weekly users, about 150k weekly issues, 60% automated resolution, and 40% human assistance.

Demand growth. User factors rise to 1.15×, 1.32×, and 1.50× across Years 1–3. Issues per user rise to 1.08×, 1.16×, and 1.25×. These are illustrative assumptions.

Case complexity. As AI resolves more routine issues, I expect the remaining human queue to include more sensitive cases and exceptions. Partners and specialists would need training, current guidance, and clear escalation paths.

Slower automation. If verified automation reaches only 75% in Year 3, about 70k issues would reach people each week, above today's 60k. I would keep coverage in place and investigate the issue mix before adjusting staffing.

Staffing decision. I would use observed case volume, coverage, backlog, customer outcomes, and cost before adjusting staffing. The forecast assigns no headcount target.

Eligibility gate. I would measure eligible issue volume by type before endorsing the 88% target. The model gives no credit for product fixes that prevent an issue from arising.

APPENDIX B / OPERATING DETAIL

How the model runs and recovers.

Keep product context and prior attempts with one issue ID.

02Resolve or route

AI completes approved work, and uncertain cases move to a person with context.

03Verify and improve

Check the result, then update guidance, tests, or product plans.

AI and product help

Owns approved guidance and actions with traceable results.

Hands off low-confidence, sensitive, or failed cases.

Partner teams

Own defined human queues, coverage, and calibrated case reviews.

Improve guidance by reporting repeat failures.

Internal specialists

Own policy, sensitive decisions, AI release rules, and partner standards.

Drive repeat-issue fixes with Product and Engineering.

Severe AI error

Stop that action, move affected cases to people, and review the cause.

More repeat contact

Freeze expansion and repair guidance or tools before another test.

Human queues stay high

Keep coverage and revisit the case mix, handoff, and staffing forecast.

Partner transition

Measure partners by lasting resolution, reliable coverage, and useful issue feedback. Retrain and move people into harder queues and review before changing contracted volume.

My approach Diagnose demand and case complexity, design the operating model, and deliver verified gains in stages.
