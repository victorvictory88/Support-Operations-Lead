CORE 01 / THE CASE FOR CHANGE

Support at 1 billion weekly users

Lots of organizations deal with scale. Fewer deal with scale and hypergrowth.The exercise starts with 150,000 support issues each week. People handle 60,000 of them.My three-year planning scenario reaches 500,000 weekly issues while AI cuts the share handled by people from 40% today to 10% in Year 3.

If automation stays at today's 60% rate while demand grows, vendor support costs rise. The high case saves about $138M in future vendor costs, or $118M after $20M in assumed rollout spending. The medium case saves about $42M. See the three outcomes.

“Support has never really been about replying to just tickets. It’s about whether people get what they need, whether it actually serves them well.”

Glen Worthington, Head of User Ops
The three-year vision

AI transforms Support from a queue that responds to issues into a connected system that anticipates needs, resolves approved problems inside the product, and learns from every exception.

My recommendation

Plan toward 90% automated resolution by Year 3 as a stretch target, which would reduce the human share of weekly issues from 40% to 10%. Move trained people into complex cases, AI review, and repeat-issue fixes first. Reduce planned vendor seats once we reach our agreed Service Level Agreement (SLA) target and service coverage stays strong.

The starting figures come from the exercise. The three-year path is my planning scenario, separate from any OpenAI target.

CORE 03 / THE PLAN

Change the product, people, and process together.

First remove repeat issues at the source, then expand approved AI actions. Keep specialist coverage and quality checks in place as the customer mix changes.

PRODUCT

Resolve more inside the product

  • Repeat-issue fixes from Product, Engineering, and Product Engagement
  • Approved in-product help and actions from AI
PEOPLE

Give people the harder work

  • Partners handle defined queues and review AI exceptions
  • Internal specialists own complex cases and AI quality
  • Trust & Safety reviews sensitive decisions
PROCESS

Run one learning loop

  • Operations links issue history across AI, partners, and specialists
  • One scorecard guides coaching, vendor decisions, and Product fixes
First 90 days
  • Audit: Baseline demand, friction, vendors, staffing, tools, and skills
  • Align: Owners, scorecards, and one urgent service gap
  • Scale: Pilot invoice lookup and product guidance, coach adoption, and set next-quarter priorities
Year 1
  • Put approved AI help inside high-volume product journeys
  • Link Support Delivery, Product, and Trust & Safety decisions
  • Plan about 1,030 vendor seat equivalents as demand grows
Year 2
  • Expand approved AI actions across products and channels
  • Route repeat failures into Product fixes and partner coaching
  • Bring modeled vendor capacity near today's level, about 930 seats
Year 3
  • Run AI as the first route for eligible support needs
  • Keep specialists on complex cases, policy, and AI quality
  • Plan about 780 vendor seat equivalents after SLA and coverage checks

Vendor seats are modeled capacity, not OpenAI staffing. The high case assumes about 940 seat equivalents today, 90% of human cases handled by vendors, and 3,000 cases per rep each year.

Check every quarter

QualityDurable resolution, repeat contact, severe AI errors, tailored guidance

ProductivityAutomated resolution, approved AI tool use, time to resolution

CapacityCoverage breadth and depth, backlog age, attrition, no-shows

Cost to ServeCost per durable resolution, vendor spend, AI run cost, rework

In quarterly top-to-top reviews, OpenAI and each vendor's leadership would assess these four areas against shared targets. For any gap, I would agree on a vendor-specific plan with an owner, milestones, and a follow-up date.

I assumed $20M in additional AI use, tooling, training, and quality checks over three years, with $4M, $6M, and $10M in Years 1–3. This is a round planning input, not a vendor quote or an OpenAI budget. Finance would price the two 90-day pilots before setting a budget.

The model already uses 30% onshore and 70% offshore vendor costs ?. That mix is included in every case. See the inputs and public price references.

Separate location option

Moving 100 suitable vendor seats from onshore to offshore could save about $7.0M yearly before transition costs. That is separate from AI savings. The high case starts with about 940 modeled vendor seats, and offshore shifts carry quality risk in language, specialist accuracy, and escalation.

What I need from leadership
  • Two pilot journeys. Approve invoice lookup and product how-to guidance as the first low-risk AI tests, with human escalation available.
  • Interoperability alignment. Assign Product, Engineering, Trust & Safety, Support Delivery, Operations, and Product Engagement to one issue history and fast decisions.
  • A priced pilot budget. Ask Finance to cost the two pilots so AI usage and quality checks have a clear limit.
How I would judge progress
  • Day 90. Target 10% fewer eligible cases reaching people, a 5-point gain in durable resolution, and zero severe AI errors in the two pilots.
  • Each year. Check automation share against 70%, 82%, and 90%, alongside human share of 30%, 18%, and 10%.
  • Each quarter. Require repeat contact and backlog age at or below baseline, plus vendor savings confirmed by Finance.
REWARD

If quality and existing vendor arrangements support the seat plan, the high case saves about $118M net across three years.

RISK AND RESPONSE

Wrong AI answers or staffing limits can erase savings. Stop an affected AI path after a severe error and keep human coverage if queues rise.

Starting point. The exercise supplies 1 billion weekly users, about 150k weekly issues, and a 60% automated resolution rate.

Demand growth. This stretch case assumes weekly users double over three years. Paid, business, and developer use can also raise issue frequency from 1.5 to 2.5 per 10,000 weekly users. Together those assumptions produce 500k weekly issues in Year 3. The yearly steps are planning estimates, and I would replace them with observed segment demand.

Human case mix. As AI resolves more common issues, the remaining cases may need more judgment, current guidance, and stronger escalation paths.

Slower AI progress. The low case holds Year 3 AI resolution at the Year 2 stretch target of 82%, leaving about 90k weekly cases for people. This is an illustrative downside, not a forecast.

AI-ready issues. Before expanding AI, I would confirm the volume of issues it can handle safely with approved answers or actions, then check that customers stay helped.

Staffing. Modeled vendor seat equivalents rise from about 940 today to 1,030 in Year 1, then fall to 930 in Year 2 and 780 in Year 3. These are workload estimates, not current OpenAI headcount. Actual staffing depends on case mix, SLA, quality, and existing vendor arrangements.
