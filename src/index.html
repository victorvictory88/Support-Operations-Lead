CORE 01 / THE CASE FOR CHANGE

Lots of organizations deal with scale.Fewer deal with scale and hypergrowth.

The exercise starts with 1 billion weekly users and 150,000 support issues each week. People handle 60,000 of those issues.My three-year planning scenario reaches 500,000 weekly issues while AI cuts the share handled by people from 40% today to 10% in Year 3.

Imagine a World Where:

2 billionweekly users
500kweekly issues
90%automated resolution
17%fewer modeled vendor reps
The three-year vision

AI transforms Support from a queue that just responds to issues, to a connected system that 1) anticipates needs, 2) resolves approved problems inside the product, and 3) learns from every exception.

My recommendationPotential $111M
  1. Build an interoperable Support system. Make the structural investment in shared issue history, approved guidance, and quality checks across Product, Sales, Support, and partners. Use a feedback loop to turn recurring issues into Product fixes.
  2. Expand AI and customer self-service in the product. Test invoice/billing reconciliation plus product guidance, then teach customers to resolve eligible needs before escalation.
  3. Put Support expertise closer to the customer. Embed specialists with vendor sales teams, train partners for harder cases and AI review, and adjust staffing after SLA and coverage goals hold.

The starting figures come from the exercise. The three-year path is my planning scenario, separate from any OpenAI target.

CORE 03 / STRATEGIC PRIORITIES

Three investments make the stretch case possible.

Build the process foundation first, add AI product journeys as they pass quality checks, and develop people for harder cases and customer enablement throughout the plan.

01 · PROCESS FOUNDATION

Build interoperability into Support

$6MThree-year assumption · $2M each year

  • Operations links one issue history across AI, vendor sites, Sales, Product, and Support
  • Support Delivery and Trust & Safety maintain guidance, quality checks, and escalation rules

Why invest Linked records and checks reveal gaps before AI errors become repeat contacts.

02 · PRODUCT JOURNEYS

Resolve more inside the product

$10MYear 1 $1M · Year 2 $3M · Year 3 $6M

  • Product and Engineering connect account context to approved AI help and actions
  • AI and reps teach customers to complete eligible tasks before escalation, starting with invoice/billing reconciliation and product guidance

Why invest The 90% stretch needs useful self-service and completed actions as demand grows.

03 · PEOPLE AND PARTNERS

Put specialists at vendor sites

$4MYear 1 $1M · Year 2 $1M · Year 3 $2M

  • Support Delivery embeds specialists with vendor sales reps for faster resolution and a direct feedback loop
  • Train partners for complex cases, AI review, and coaching customers to use approved self-service

Why invest Customers need skilled people for judgment, coverage, and guidance as routine volume shifts.

The $20M split is an illustrative planning assumption pending Finance pricing. Spending continues each year because issue data, AI use, quality checks, embedded specialist coverage, and training continue as volume grows.

First 90 days

  • Align: Set shared issue history, owners, and pilot success measures
  • Test: Run invoice/billing reconciliation and product guidance with human escalation
Year 1 · prove
  • Release checked AI help and customer self-service in high-volume journeys
  • Embed Support specialists at pilot vendor sites and certify partner handoffs
  • Target 70% automated resolution and plan about 1,030 modeled vendor reps
Year 2 · expand
  • Add approved AI actions and customer guidance across products and channels
  • Feed vendor-site and customer friction into knowledge and Product fixes

Year 3 · redesign

  • Focus vendor reps and specialists on complex cases, enablement, and quality review
  • Target 90% automated resolution and plan about 780 vendor reps if SLA and coverage hold

Day 90 decision. Expand if 10% fewer eligible cases reach people, checked resolution improves five points, severe AI errors remain at zero, and SLA and backlog stay on target.

At 500k weekly issues, 60% AI resolution leaves 200k cases for people. The 90% case leaves 50k. The 150k weekly difference is the volume behind the high case. Finance must confirm lower vendor spending before counting savings.

The model sets aside an illustrative $20M across three years for AI use, product integration, training, and quality checks. The yearly planning amounts are $4M, $6M, and $10M as rollout grows. Finance would price the two 90-day pilots and replace this estimate before an investment decision.

The model already uses 30% onshore and 70% offshore vendor costs ?. That mix is included in every case. See the inputs and public price references.

What I need from leadership
  • Approve two bounded pilots. Test invoice/billing reconciliation and product guidance with human escalation, then check customer outcomes at Day 90.
  • Approve the interoperability model. Assign owners across Product, Engineering, Trust & Safety, Support Delivery, Operations, and Product Engagement for shared issue history, AI actions, and embedded specialists at vendor sites.
  • Price the three-year investment. Ask Finance to validate the $20M Process, Product, and People split before approving wider rollout.
Main risks and my response
  • AI gives a harmful or incomplete answer. Stop the affected action, route cases to people, and repair the guidance before restarting.
  • AI gains fall short of plan. Lower the 90% target and keep human coverage if fewer issues can be resolved safely.
  • Savings stay in the model. Count savings only after Finance confirms vendor spend can fall while SLA and coverage goals hold.

Starting point. The exercise supplies 1 billion weekly users, about 150k weekly issues, and a 60% automated resolution rate.

Demand stress case. This path assumes weekly users double and issue frequency rises from 1.5 to 2.5 per 10,000 users as customer needs expand. That produces 500k weekly issues in Year 3. Product fixes could keep issue frequency lower, so I would update this path each quarter.

If Product reduces issues. If issue frequency falls to 1.25 per 10,000 users by Year 3, 2 billion users generate about 250k weekly issues. With the high-case AI and vendor assumptions, net savings fall from $111M to about $51M. Total issues still grow because weekly users double. To fall below today's 150k issues, frequency would need to drop below 0.75 per 10,000 users.

Case mix. Separate repeatable questions, approved account actions, complex billing and access cases, and sensitive policy or safety cases. AI takes approved routine issues. Trained partners and specialists take the harder remainder.

90% feasibility. Automated resolution counts all started issues that finish without a person, excluding abandonment. As a math check, 95% of issues would need to be safe for automation and 95% of those would need a checked resolution to reach about 90% overall. If the first audit finds a smaller eligible share, I would lower the target and keep human coverage.

Slower AI progress. The low case reaches 70% automated resolution in Year 2 and stalls there in Year 3, leaving about 150k weekly cases for people. This downside is an illustrative scenario.

Staffing. The exercise gives no vendor rep count. Every financial case assigns 90% of human cases to vendors. Year 3 uses 3,000 because the remaining human cases may take longer. Case logs, SLA, and quality results would set the staffing plan.

APPENDIX B / OPERATING DETAIL

One issue history from start to finish.

In this illustrative case, a customer sees a card charge that differs from their invoice.

01Capture the question

The customer selects the charge in the product. One issue record keeps the invoice number, charge date, amount, and help steps already tried.

02Resolve or hand off

AI explains an approved match between the charge and invoice. If the amounts differ or the answer is uncertain, a billing specialist receives the full record and checks the account.

03Check and improve

Operations checks the outcome and any repeat contact. If many customers ask about one billing label, Product changes that label and Support updates AI guidance.

Embedded Support specialists at vendor sites
  • Join vendor sales reps on complex billing, access, and product questions while keeping the customer issue history intact.
  • Coach reps to show customers approved AI help and self-service steps before escalation.
  • Tag recurring blockers in the shared issue record, then review fixes weekly with Support Delivery, Product Engagement, Operations, and Product.

Pilot this role at selected vendor sites and expand coverage only when resolution speed, repeat contact, and SLA improve.

How we check the quarterly scorecard
  • Quality. During the two 90-day pilots, review at least 100 randomly selected AI closures per journey each week. Check the requested outcome and seven-day repeat contact. A severe privacy, policy, or access error stops that AI action.
  • Productivity. At Day 90, aim for 10% fewer eligible issues reaching people and a five-point increase in checked resolution.
  • Capacity. Keep the agreed SLA and backlog targets on track before expanding either journey.
  • Cost to Serve. Count vendor savings after Finance confirms future spending falls. These checks supply evidence for the four measures above.

Separate location option

Moving 100 suitable vendor reps from onshore to offshore could save about $7M yearly before transition costs. That is separate from AI savings. The high case starts with about 940 modeled vendor reps, and offshore shifts carry quality risk in language, specialist accuracy, and escalation.

What breakeven looks like
  • AI resolves roughly 62%, 63%, and 65% of weekly issues across Years 1–3. In Year 3, people still handle about 175,000 of 500,000 issues each week.
  • With the shared vendor assumptions, Year 3 needs about 2,730 vendor reps. Holding AI at 60% would need about 3,120.
  • Those rounded AI rates save about $22M in vendor cost over three years, just above the assumed $20M investment. SLA, coverage, and resolution quality still need to hold.

The high case saves about $131M in vendor costs against the flat-60% AI forecast and spends an assumed $20M on AI and rollout, leaving about $111M in modeled savings.
