01 / SUMMARIZE THE SITUATION

Support at 1 billion weekly users

The exercise starts with 150,000 issues each week. Automation resolves 60%, leaving 60,000 issues for people. Growth and a harder case mix will raise human effort even if the number of human cases grows slowly.

1 billionweekly users
150kweekly issues
60%automated resolution
60khuman-assisted issues
The decision for leadership

Invest in a support system that resolves more issues where customers already are, keeps skilled people available for harder cases, and turns repeat failures into product fixes.

The four starting figures come from the hypothetical take-home exercise. My growth and improvement figures below are scenario assumptions.

02 / THE IDEA

Diagnose · Design · Deliver

Diagnose the full customer journey and the capacity left after automation. Design a shared system for product help, partner teams, and internal specialists. Deliver in stages, expanding only when customer outcomes and operating capacity support the change.

01

Diagnose

Connect the issue, channel, answer, handoff, repeat contact, and final customer outcome. Forecast effort by case type instead of using ticket counts alone.

02

Design

Put tested help inside the product, give partners clear queues and quality rules, and reserve internal specialists for judgment and product defects.

03

Deliver

Pilot two common journeys, review matched outcomes, train backup coverage, and move recurring failure patterns into Product and Engineering.

The vision What this looks like in 3 years

A customer gets useful help in the product, and the issue history follows them if a person steps in. Support teams, partners, and product owners use one view of quality, effort, and repeat demand. Specialists spend more time on complex cases and on improving the system.

Where to play

Start with high-volume, low-risk journeys where the customer need and outcome can be measured. Keep sensitive, unclear, or high-risk issues with trained people.

How to win

Connect every support step to the customer outcome, then improve the knowledge, routing, tooling, and partner coaching behind it.

03 / HOW IT WORKS · DEMAND AND CAPABILITY

Human cases level off while effort keeps rising.

I use a three-year planning case to show the capacity implications. By Year 3, weekly users reach 1.5× today's exercise baseline, issues per user reach 1.25×, and a human case takes 1.3× today's average effort. Verified automated resolution rises from 60% to 75% in stages.

Why these assumptions: User growth expands the base. More products and business use cases can lift issue frequency. Automation can remove easier cases first, leaving a harder human queue. The multipliers illustrate those pressures and a measured improvement path; they come from my judgment, separate from any OpenAI forecast.

Capacity and capability trend

Human case volume rises modestly in this planning case, while effort climbs about 52% from today's baseline. I would add specialized skills, better tools, and trained flexible coverage before translating effort units into staffing. Forecasting would use handle time by case type, qualified hours, occupancy, shrinkage, and service windows.

03 / HOW IT WORKS · PRIORITIES AND OPERATING MODEL

Three investments make the model usable.

01

One issue record

Join channel, automation, partner, specialist, and repeat-contact events. This makes customer outcomes and contribution visible by issue type.

Trade-off Data integration comes before broad automation expansion.
02

Knowledge and evaluations

Turn frontline failures into updated guidance, test cases, and product fixes. Roll out low-risk journeys with sampled review and an easy human handoff.

Trade-off Controlled pilots grow slower, with stronger evidence.
03

Partner capability

Give partners clear routing, calibrated quality, training, and trained backup staffing. Shift incentives toward durable resolution and lower rework.

Trade-off Coverage and training cost precede productivity gains.

Who does what

Product help and AI

Handles approved, repeatable requests within clear limits.

Owns a complete history, a visible handoff route, and traceable answers.

Improves through evaluations, content changes, and product fixes.

Partner teams

Handle repeatable human cases across approved queues and time zones.

Own staffing, coaching, documentation, and trained backup coverage.

Improve through audited outcomes and issue-pattern feedback.

Internal specialists

Handle sensitive issues, unclear policy, incidents, and product defects.

Own routing rules, quality calibration, forecasts, partner governance, and final escalation decisions.

Improve the knowledge and product backlog with Product and Engineering.

OpenAI would set policy, evaluation rules, launch gates, and partner contracts. Partners would execute within those rules. A severe privacy, fraud, policy, or access error would route to a human owner and stop the affected automated path until reviewed.

04 / BENEFIT · MEASURES OF SUCCESS

Judge the full customer outcome, then the contributors.

The lead measure is durable resolution by issue type. The customer gets a correct answer with no repeat contact about that issue within the appropriate review window. A shared issue ID lets leadership see what automation, partners, and internal specialists each contributed.

QuestionMeasures to reviewWhat would change my plan
Did the customer get help?Durable resolution, repeat contact, audited answer quality, severe errors, time to resolution, and customer effort.Rising recontact or severe errors would stop expansion on that route and trigger case review.
Can capacity keep up?Arrivals by issue type, qualified hours, backlog age, occupancy, handle time, partner attrition, no-show rate, and late rate.Persistent aged backlog would shift schedules, trained backup staffing, or specialist capacity.
Does each step add value?Eligible automation use, verified automation resolution, handoff quality, partner durable resolution, specialist rework, and cost per durable resolution.Higher automation use with weaker outcomes would send the journey back to design.
Where is repeat demand coming from?Issue coverage across products and channels, journey starts, successful routing, resolution, and repeat-contact clusters.Recurring clusters would move into a Product and Engineering fix queue.
How I would adapt my Meta scorecard

Revenue. Gross and incremental revenue help prioritize business customer pain, while durable resolution remains the Support goal.

Coverage. Breadth becomes product, channel, and issue coverage. Depth becomes qualified staffing for each queue.

Solution score. Pitched calls per book and solutions per call become the share of eligible issues with a relevant next step and a verified outcome.

Quality. Tailored solutions and commercial conversations become answer relevance, context use, and clear ownership for business customers.

Workforce. Attrition, no-shows, and late arrivals still show staffing reliability. Very short contacts prompt a quality sample.

Journey funnel. Attempted, connected, pitched, and actioned become issue start, routing, guidance delivered, and confirmed resolution. Segment by entry channel.

Tooling. Ads AI adoption becomes support-tool use among eligible agents, paired with audited quality and customer outcomes.

Compare like-for-like issue types, risk, channel, and customer groups. Show total customer outcomes plus automation, partner, and internal specialist contributions; a transfer or reopened issue should remain visible across all three.

05 / CLOSE · SEQUENCE AND ACTION PLAN

Build the evidence, then expand the useful paths.

First 90 days

Join issue and outcome data, set the quality and capacity baseline, choose two low-risk journeys, and pilot with human review.

Year 1

Improve product help and partner routing on proven journeys. Calibrate quality across partners and build trained backup coverage.

Year 2

Expand proven paths across more products and channels. Move recurring case clusters into Product and Engineering roadmaps.

Year 3

Operate one linked support system, with specialists focused on complex cases and partners handling well-defined queues at greater scale.

Stop

Stop a route after a severe privacy, fraud, policy, or access error.

Start

Start a small pilot when its issue type, owner, outcome, and human route are defined.

Continue

Continue staged expansion when durable resolution improves and risk and backlog stay controlled.

APPENDIX / SCENARIO ASSUMPTIONS

The planning assumptions behind this recommendation.

Starting point. The exercise supplies 1 billion weekly users, about 150k weekly issues, 60% automated resolution, and 40% human assistance.

Demand growth. Weekly users rise to 1.15×, 1.32×, and 1.50× across Years 1–3. Issues per user rise to 1.08×, 1.16×, and 1.25×. These illustrative steps are my planning assumptions.

Case complexity. Human effort per case rises to 1.10×, 1.20×, and 1.30× because repeatable issues may leave the human queue first. One effort unit equals today's average human case.

Automation path. Verified resolution rises to 65%, 70%, and 75% only in this planning case. The exercise provides no target. Count an issue as resolved only after an appropriate repeat-contact window and quality check.

Capacity conversion. The model estimates effort before translating it into headcount. Staffing requires handle time, qualified hours, occupancy, shrinkage, case mix, and service windows.

Contribution and risk. A linked issue record attributes each step while preserving the final customer outcome. Human owners review severe errors and expansion decisions.

External source: OpenAI's published support model. Its discussion of support surfaces, knowledge, evaluations, and frontline feedback informs the operating design.
