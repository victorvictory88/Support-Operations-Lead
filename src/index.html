CORE 01 / THE CASE FOR CHANGE

Support at 1 billion weekly users

Lots of organizations deal with scale. Fewer deal with scale and hypergrowth.The exercise starts with 150,000 support issues each week. People handle 60,000 of them.My three-year planning scenario reaches 500,000 weekly issues while AI cuts the share handled by people from 40% today to 10% in Year 3.

THREE-YEAR STRETCH CASE~$118M

As support demand grows, keeping automation at today's 60% rate would require more vendor support. In the high case, greater automation saves about $138M in future vendor costs. After $20M in assumed AI and rollout spending, net savings are about $118M. See the three outcomes.

“Support has never really been about replying to just tickets. It’s about whether people get what they need, whether it actually serves them well.”

Glen Worthington, Head of User Ops
The three-year vision

AI transforms Support from a queue that responds to issues into a connected system that anticipates needs, resolves approved problems inside the product, and learns from every exception.

My recommendation

Plan toward 90% automated resolution by Year 3 as a stretch target, which would reduce the human share of weekly issues from 40% to 10%. Move trained people into complex cases, AI review, and repeat-issue fixes first. Reduce planned vendor seats once we reach our agreed Service Level Agreement (SLA) target ? and service coverage stays strong.

The starting figures come from the exercise. The three-year path is my planning scenario, separate from any OpenAI target.

CORE 03 / THE PLAN

Change the product, people, and process together.

PRODUCT

Resolve more inside the product

  • Repeat-issue fixes from Product and Product Engagement
  • Approved in-product help and actions from AI
PEOPLE

Give people the harder work

  • Specialist and partner training through Support Delivery
  • AI-prepared context for complex handoffs
  • Trust & Safety review for sensitive decisions
PROCESS

Run one learning loop

  • Linked issue history and quality checks through Operations
  • AI signals for coaching, vendor reviews, and Product fixes

CORE 04 / THE FINANCIAL CHOICE

A $118M high case, with clear funding gates.

Every case uses one demand forecast, with 220k, 330k, and 500k weekly issues across Years 1–3. Each compares vendor costs with the cost of keeping automation at today's 60% rate as demand grows. The net figures subtract assumed AI, tooling, training, and quality-check spending. Revenue growth stays outside this estimate.

The $20M rollout figure is a planning estimate for AI use, tools, training, and quality checks across three years. It allocates $4M to Year 1, $6M to Year 2, and $10M to Year 3. Finance would set actual budgets after pricing the pilots.

The model already uses 30% onshore and 70% offshore vendor costs ?. That mix is included in every case. See the inputs and public price references.

Separate location option

Moving 100 suitable vendor seats from onshore to offshore could save about $7.0M per year before transition costs. That is separate from AI savings and carries potential quality risk in language, specialist accuracy, and escalation.

What I need from leadership
  • Issue and cost data. Confirm which cases AI can resolve and which vendor costs can change.
  • Named partners. Product, Engineering, Trust & Safety, Support Delivery, Operations, and Product Engagement can clear pilot decisions quickly.
  • A capped pilot budget. Price two pilot journeys with Finance, then release further funding after results support expansion.
How I would judge progress
  • Day 90. Target 10% fewer eligible cases reaching people, a 5-point gain in durable resolution, and zero severe AI errors.
  • Each year. Check automation share against 70%, 82%, and 90%, alongside human share of 30%, 18%, and 10%.
  • Each quarter. Require repeat contact and backlog age at or below baseline, plus vendor savings confirmed by Finance.
REWARD

If quality holds and contracts adjust, the high case saves about $118M net across three years.

RISK AND RESPONSE

Wrong AI answers or rigid contracts can erase savings. Stop the affected AI path after a severe error, keep human coverage if queues rise, and release funding in stages.

Starting point. The exercise supplies 1 billion weekly users, about 150k weekly issues, and a 60% automated resolution rate.

Demand growth. I use matching growth factors for users and issues per user. The underlying factors are square roots of each year's issue total divided by 150k, which keeps the displayed volumes consistent after rounding.

Human case mix. As AI resolves more common issues, the remaining cases may need more judgment, current guidance, and stronger escalation paths.

Slower AI progress. At 82% AI resolution in Year 3, about 90k weekly cases still reach people. I would keep coverage until the issue mix and service levels support a change.

AI-ready issues. Before expanding AI, I would confirm the volume of issues it can handle safely with approved answers or actions, then check that customers stay helped.

Staffing. This scenario sets case-share targets and leaves headcount open. Vendor plans follow observed demand, quality, coverage, and contract terms.

Stop
